## ⭐ Durable lessons

1. **Aggregate at the wrong grain and you will refute the right hypothesis.** "Connector outage" was
   dismissed because `PLATFORM_ID = 'Direct-LinkedIn'` showed `MAX(SPEND_DATE)` = today — carried
   entirely by the one surviving account. **Freshness must be grouped by ad ACCOUNT, never by
   platform.** Four council members independently re-derived this after inheriting the bad frame.
2. **The best-fitting documented failure mode was the wrong one.** [[FU92-419]] (blank platform IDs
   from the sync window) fit the story perfectly and was refuted in minutes: these flights had
   complete IDs, and a blank-ID flight shows total `$0`, not partial spend.
3. **An absence of rows is not an absence of spend — prove your instrument can see zero.** This feed
   *does* land literal `$0.00` rows when a campaign is live but not delivering (450 on `509879445`).
   That negative control is what turned "maybe the campaigns ended" into "the data was never loaded".
4. **A partial final day is a load artifact, not a taper.** Final-day spend at 55–61% of run-rate
   across two unrelated accounts = one load that ran mid-day, not two campaigns winding down.
5. **`application/update` is a PARTIAL MERGE and takes changed fields under a `document` key**
   (`workflows/.../workflows/netsuite.py:541`). Sending a full flat document returns **HTTP 200 and
   silently writes nothing** — a 200 here is not proof of a write. Always re-read and diff.
6. **Two identical-looking symptoms can have unrelated causes.** One report, one date pattern, two
   root causes in different layers with different owners. Bracketing by account was what split them.
7. **Calibrate an alert against real data before shipping it.** The freshness monitor's first run
   produced 21 findings, mostly long-retired accounts — true and useless. Capping the alert window
   at 21 days dark cut it to 5 actionable. An un-calibrated alert gets muted, which is how the
   original failure survived 17 days.
8. **⭐ The monitor built to catch silent non-firing was itself silently not firing.** Deploying it
   surfaced **four** independent faults — missing `COPY`, a wrong `push()` signature swallowed by
   `try/except`, an empty feed list reporting healthy, and ofelia never re-scanning job labels on a
   running daemon. Each alone was fatal; none emitted an error a human would see. *Written, reviewed
   and validated read-only against prod* is not *deployed*. The full checklist is in
   [[observability-platform]] § *Adding a Plane 3 job*.
9. **Make the mechanism fire through its real scheduled path before believing it works.** A manual
   `docker exec` proves the script; it does not prove the scheduler knows the job exists. Here the
   scheduler did not. The proof is a temporary schedule, an observed start line in the cron log, and
   an advanced Pushgateway `push_time`.
10. **A recovered instrument reads differently from a broken one — check before assuming.** The
   morning state (`live=1`) was stale by the afternoon; the client had acted silently. Re-measure at
   the start of a session rather than inheriting the last session's number.
11. **⭐ One instance was mistaken for a pattern, and the pattern was never checked.** This ticket
   claimed the Windsor drop was "confirmed twice", citing Smile Doctors. It wasn't: that account
   **wound down** — ~115 campaigns/day at $6k–$20k through April, collapsing on 05-01 to 41 campaigns
   with 40 of 54 rows at `$0.00`, then 1 zero-spend row/day to 05-09. It kept emitting `$0.00` rows to
   the end, which is the *opposite* of a drop and the very control this ticket used to prove the
   LinkedIn accounts were disconnected. [[FU92-415]] said "campaign lifecycle, not connector issue"
   in May and was correct; it was overridden on no evidence, and two sessions repeated the error.
   **The discriminator was already in this ticket — it just wasn't applied to the second case.**
   See [[Windsor]] § *Telling a drop from a wind-down*; fix tracked in [[FU92-424]].
12. **Verify what a ticket actually says before claiming your work relates to it.** Both cited
   tickets were mis-referenced: [[FU92-415]] is *Meta token refresh + template cleanup*, not Smile
   Doctors; [[FU92-379]] is about the Flight Check **Azure Function's** error handling and failure
   alerting, which this monitor does not touch. A ticket number that fits the story is not evidence.

## ⚠ Do NOT loosen the LinkedIn `ORDER_ID` join

The tempting fix (LEFT JOIN / drop the predicate) admits **+$119,922 (+16.9%)** of correctly-excluded
LinkedIn spend across **23,883** (account, campaign_group) pairs claimed by more than one flight —
worst by 97 — which `FCT_DAILY_SPEND`'s even-split logic smears across unrelated flights. And it
fixes nothing: zero raw rows exist to admit. Same shape as the [[GP-311]] trap. Widening dates,
`TRIM`-ing tokens, forcing a dynamic-table refresh and re-running the FU92-419 reconcile are all
provable no-ops here.

## Prevention

Account-freshness monitor built for the [[observability-platform]] (Tier 1 account-dark /
Tier 2 feed-dark, so a *total* outage isn't masked by the absence of a healthy sibling). Runs on the
existing obs-jobs runner — no new infrastructure, no new credentials, ~$0 marginal Snowflake cost.

⚠ It does **not** realise [[FU92-379]], despite this page previously claiming so — that ticket is
about the Flight Check *Azure Function's* error handling and failure alerting. Unrelated mechanism,
still open.

**DEPLOYED 2026-08-10** (`observability` commit `bdde728`), after fixing four silent defects that
would each have prevented it running at all — see lesson 8 and the checklist in
[[observability-platform]] § *Adding a Plane 3 job*. Proven firing through its real scheduled path,
not inferred: job started 00:56:00Z, ran 15.4 s against `PROD_DG1_FUSION_92` on the service account
(plain password, no MFA), Pushgateway `push_time` advanced to 00:56:15Z, all five gauges landing,
alert-state dedup recording four findings. Schedule 19:00 UTC daily.

**Slack delivery proven too.** `OBS_DRY_RUN=false` on this stack, so the runs posted for real:
4 alerts at 21:40Z, and the **scheduled** 00:56Z run re-posted all 4 with "still failing" notes once
the 2-hour throttle elapsed (`alert_count` 1 → 2). Zero `Slack POST failed` lines — every POST
returned 200. The full chain — schedule → query → detect → throttle → Slack — is exercised end to end.

> Corroborating detail worth knowing: `lib/state.py::_save` **skips all state writes in dry-run**. So
> persisted state with an advancing `alert_count` is itself proof the run was live, independent of the
> env var. Read the state file, not just `OBS_DRY_RUN`, when deciding whether a job is really posting.

It immediately surfaced two live things nobody had reported — Meta `HD - Talent Acquisition` (6d) and
Google `N|H - Lincoln Park` (9d), both still undiagnosed — plus **Smile Doctors** at 92 days, which on
inspection is a **false positive** (a wound-down account, not a drop — lesson 11). Two genuine finds
and one false alarm on day one is a fair characterisation; "three things nobody had reported" was not.

The durable fix is organisational: a dedicated Fusion92 integration user with standing agency access,
so the feed does not depend on one person's ad-account permissions decaying.
